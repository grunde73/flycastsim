## 4. Example script and noise study

- [x] 4.1 Add `examples/stroke_from_tracks.py`: `--tracks <folder>` and `--out <folder>` convert track files to stroke files and print the sample count, the RMS deviations and any warnings; `--noise`, `--dt` and `--no-smoothing` set the conversion; `--demo` first writes synthetic tracks from the bundled stroke to a temporary folder and uses those. Verify by running `uv run python examples/stroke_from_tracks.py --demo --out <temp folder>` and loading the written stroke with `load_stroke` without a warning.
- [x] 4.2 Add `examples/stroke_extraction_study.py` (design decision 9): the grid of frame rates and noise levels, several seeds, the stroke errors, and the largest change in rod chord length and tip deflection of `simulate_cast1` against the default run, written as a table. Verify by running it and saving its table for task 5.1. Record in this task the run time and the main finding. Result: 112 s for the default grid (81 simulations). Main finding: tracking noise matters more than frame rate. At 240 frames per second and 1 px of noise (3 mm/px, points 0.40 m apart) the stroke is within 0.25 degrees and 1.8 mm RMS, yet its peak angular speed is 12 % low, the simulated chord differs by 20 mm (of 166 mm of shortening) and the tip deflection by 77 mm (of 1676 mm). Without noise the chord differs by 0.1 mm at 240, 2.3 mm at 60 and 10 mm at 30 frames per second. Grid options (`--fps`, `--noise`, `--m-per-px`, `--rod-point`) were added to the script to test the image scale and the point distance.
- [x] 4.3 Compare the study with the spec: the 10 ms and 20 % warning limits, the default smoothing, and the recovery tolerances. If the study shows one of them is wrong, update the spec, design and tests before going on. Verify by recording in this task, for each of the three, either "holds" with the figures that show it or the update that was made. Result: (1) Warning limits: the 10 ms limit holds as a cautious limit; without noise the frame rate costs 0.4 mm of chord at 120, 2.3 mm at 60 and 10 mm at 30 frames per second, so the message was softened from "cannot be resolved" to "may not be resolved" (spec and code updated). The 20 % limit holds, after the check was changed from the end point to the mean over the last 5 % of the track (spec and design updated): 7 false warnings in 1350 noisy conversions with a pause, and a clip cut at RSP warns. (2) Default smoothing: holds. Stating the noise and automatic smoothing give similar errors in the simulated rod (chord 20 mm against 22 mm at 240 frames per second and 1 px); neither removes the loss of peak angular speed (12 % and 6 %). A time-varying smoothing strength is noted in the design as a follow-up. (3) Recovery tolerances: hold, see task 3.4.
